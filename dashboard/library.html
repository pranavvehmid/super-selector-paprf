Equations

(1) Role assignment. Among a player's observed deployments, primary role is the one with most scored opportunities. If no opportunities fall after warm-up, use observed raw opportunities. Ties use scored match count, then the unshrunk role signal if available, then role ID. A primary needs at least 60 scored balls and six scored matches to receive an index. Players who do not qualify are recorded with a reason. More opportunities determine deployment and sample support, not raw run or wicket superiority.

(2) Within-role scaling. For each eligible player and role, recover the unshrunk contextual signal c = Σ wⱼzⱼ from the signed capped components and the published role weights. Compute μ and sample standard deviation σ over all eligible players in that role. Define Q = 100Φ[(c−μ)/σ], where Φ is the standard normal cumulative distribution function. A flat cohort uses Q = 50. Q is a smooth reference score in (0, 100), with 50 at the role mean. It is not the percentage of actual peers defeated and not a probability of future success. Unlike averaging rank positions, the transformation retains differences in component magnitudes. Normal reference scaling reduces units and scale incompatibilities, though skew and unequal role strength can still matter.

(3) Evidence shrinkage. Define R = n/(n+120) and S = 50 + R(Q−50). This uses the existing role prior and shrinks toward neutral exactly once. Applying reliability to the already shrunken published role score would count sample size twice. At n = 120, R = 0.5. With Q = 90, S = 70. S ranges from 0 to 100 and is a historical role-relative index.

(4) Overall index. Select at most one supported secondary role with the largest credit, where credit = 0.10 × min(1, nₛ/nₚ) × max(0, Sₛ−50). Here p and s identify primary and secondary roles. Other roles remain visible without accumulating credits. The index I = min(100, Sₚ + credit). For Sₚ = 70, Sₛ = 80 and nₛ/nₚ = 0.5, credit is 1.5 and I = 71.5. The bonus is at most 5 points before clipping. Rank by score at six decimal places and break ties by numeric player ID.

Opposition quality and pressure context are already included in the expected-outcome benchmark. Pressure lift, India support, annual/recency contrast and historical bootstrap membership are shown as evidence. They receive no separate automatic bonus. That avoids rewarding the same balls twice or assigning an unvalidated India or recency multiplier. The prior forward tests do not establish stable player ordering in every role. Consistency and forecast interpretation remain limitations. Age and pitch characteristics require further verified data.

Contextual T20 role profiles

Protocol

This research iteration measures effectiveness relative to expected delivered outcomes. The historical PAPRF v4 pressure ranks remain a distinct comparison. All role and opponent estimates use the supplied organiser data.

Batting roles are based on the source batting slot and first pre-delivery phase seen as striker or non-striker in that innings. Slots 1–2 are openers, 3–4 top order and 5+ middle/lower order, except players in slots 5+ first seen in the death phase, who are classified as late entrants. This is an observed deployment proxy, not a permanent finisher designation. Bowlers are evaluated separately in each phase. A player can qualify in multiple roles. Batting uses balls faced and bowling uses legal balls. Bowling model runs use legal-ball concessions. Wides and no-ball concessions remain in the conventional economy column, assigned to the same phase, and are outside the legal-ball residual model. A boundary must have the matching four or six flag and attributed runs. A batting dismissal must occur on a counted striker ball.

Earlier-date baseline and opposition adjustment

Process whole match dates in order. Freeze all rates until the date's rows are scored, then update the rates. Keep the first 195 matches before 3 April 2023 as warm-up. For each role, define the observation vector Y = (runs, boundary indicator, dot indicator, dismissal or credited wicket indicator, six indicator). Coaching and recruitment interpretations are outside these metrics.

The context key includes innings, phase, competition family, batting deployment or bowling phase, India venue indicator, pressure band, and observed opposing style (pace/spin or batting hand). Let a historical group have outcome sum S and n opportunities. Calculate its mean as (S + k × parent mean)/(n + k). The hierarchy moves from discipline mean to deployment/phase/family parent with k = 120 and then to the full cell with k = 120. Before any role history exists, use fixed priors of 1 run, 0.15 boundaries, 0.35 dots, 0.04 dismissals or wickets and 0.05 sixes per opportunity. Priors are initialisation assumptions.

Compute earlier-date residuals as actual minus the unadjusted context expectation. Estimate a team residual mean with denominator n + 360 and an individual opponent residual mean with denominator n + 120. Use 25% team and 75% individual in the adjustment, independently on each outcome. Clip runs to [0, 6] for batting or [0, 8] for legal bowling and indicators to [0, 1]. The opponent is the delivering bowler for batting, and current striker for bowling. This is a historical proxy and does not separate individual skill, other opponents and scheduling effects. Quality tiers use the prior individual contextual run residual, oriented so harder opposition is positive. A tier requires 120 prior opportunities, with strong at +0.10 or above, weaker at −0.10 or below, average between, and unknown otherwise. Report an out-of-date prediction MSE comparison against the unadjusted model. Competition family adjustment controls observed baseline differences and is not a causal tournament-quality ranking.

Scores and dimensions

Aggregate residuals per opportunity. In each eligible role pool, z-standardise runs, boundary and event residual rates using sample standard deviations. Orient scoring and boundaries up and dismissal risk down for batters, and orient run and boundary prevention up and credited wickets up for bowlers. Clip signed z-scores at ±3. Eligibility requires 60 scored balls and six scored matches. Multiply the weighted composite by n/(n+120), with n the unweighted role opportunity count. The primary score weights all locations equally. Stress-test India at 1.5× event weight, and compare the prespecified role weights with equal weights. No arbitrary age bonus is applied.

Follow-up redundancy test

The first run showed strong scoring–boundary correlation. Keep the original primary score as the declared reference. For the follow-up sensitivity case, let x be the role pool's run residual and y its boundary residual. Replace y with y − β(x − mean(x)), where β = Σ (x − mean(x))(y − mean(y)) / Σ (x − mean(x))². Re-standardise the transformed boundary component and keep the original weights. This strips linear dependence in the fitted role cohort. It does not make the components independent or establish predictive superiority.

Pressure Definition (Step 5)

Status: GATE PASSED Version: v1, 18 November 2026

Scope and guardrail

The pressure label describes the pre-delivery match context faced by both players on a delivery. Batter and bowler performance are measured separately in Step 6; the shared context label does not make their outcomes equivalent.

The calculation reads only pre-delivery state, innings number, and the second-innings target known before that innings begins. It does not read the rated delivery's outcome, a later delivery, the match result, or the winning team.

Inputs

Continuous pressure index

All components are bounded between zero and one.

First innings

First-innings pressure uses late-innings urgency and wickets lost:

late_innings_stress = clamp((legal_balls - 72) / 48)

wicket_stress = wickets / 10

pressure_index = 0.70 × late_innings_stress + 0.30 × wicket_stress

The first 12 overs do not receive late-innings urgency. The final eight overs progressively do.

Second innings

Second-innings pressure uses chase difficulty, wickets lost, and innings progress:

required_run_rate = 6 × runs_required / max(balls_remaining, 1)

run_rate_stress = clamp((required_run_rate - 7) / 5)

progress = legal_balls / 120

pressure_index = 0.55 × run_rate_stress + 0.25 × wicket_stress + 0.20 × progress

This treats a 7-run required rate as the start of rate stress and caps the rate component at 12 runs per over. These are transparent modelling choices, not official facts.

progress = Clamp(L / 120)
wicket_stress = Clamp(W / 10)
late_innings_stress = Clamp((L - 72) / 48)
pressure_index = 0.70 × late_innings_stress + 0.30 × wicket_stress

Second innings:

required_run_rate = 6 × R / max(B, 1)
run_rate_stress = Clamp((required_run_rate - 7) / 5)
progress = Clamp(L / 120)
wicket_stress = Clamp(W / 10)
pressure_index = 0.55 × run_rate_stress + 0.25 × wicket_stress + 0.20 × progress

ordinary: index < 0.40
elevated: 0.40 ≤ index < 0.65
high: index ≥ 0.65

2. Attribution and eligibility

Rows are excluded from player-rate metrics when extras_type is other or Penalty, when a None row has non-zero extras, or when a bye/leg-bye row has non-zero batter runs. That leaves 186,363 eligible deliveries and holds out 257 ambiguous rows.

batter_runs = 0 for wide/byes/legbyes/other/Penalty; else source runs
batter_ball_faced = eligible and extras_type ≠ wide
bowler_runs = batter_runs + extras1_runs + extras_2_runs for wide or Overstep
bowler_legal_ball = eligible and source legal-ball rule

Run outs, retire outs and obstructing-the-field dismissals are not bowler wickets. Batter dots are balls faced with zero batter runs; bowler dots are eligible legal balls with zero team runs.

3. Baseline deltas

Rate(x) = x / opportunities. Eligibility requires at least 60 ordinary opportunities, 60 pressure opportunities, six ordinary matches and six pressure matches.

batter delta_RPB = pressure runs/balls − ordinary runs/balls
batter delta_dot = pressure dots/balls − ordinary dots/balls
batter delta_dismissal = pressure dismissals/balls − ordinary dismissals/balls

bowler delta_economy = 6 × pressure conceded/legal balls − 6 × ordinary conceded/legal balls
bowler delta_dot = pressure dots/legal balls − ordinary dots/legal balls
bowler delta_wicket = pressure wickets/legal balls − ordinary wickets/legal balls

Better batting has positive RPB and negative dot/dismissal deltas. Better bowling has negative economy and positive dot/wicket deltas.

4. Standardisation, weighting and shrinkage

For delta j within an eligible role-specific peer population:

z_j = (delta_j − mean(delta_j of eligible peers)) / sample_sd(delta_j of eligible peers)
batter_raw = (z_RPB − z_dot − z_dismissal) / 3
bowler_raw = (−z_economy + z_dot + z_wicket) / 3
shrinkage_weight = pressure_opportunities / (pressure_opportunities + 60)
reliability_signal = raw_signal × shrinkage_weight

The freeze script sorts the reliability_signal stored to six decimal places in the signal CSV. Equal stored scores preserve the input row order. Charts display two decimals. IDs are the identity key.

5. Sensitivity mathematics

For prior P in {30, 60, 90}:

alternative_signal(P) = raw_signal × pressure_opportunities / (pressure_opportunities + P)

Top-10 overlap is the size of the intersection between the official P=60 top ten and each alternative top ten. A high-only test rebuilds eligibility, peer distributions and signals using high pressure only. Results: 10/10 batter overlap for each prior; bowler overlap is 9/10 at P=30 and 10/10 at P=60/90; high-only overlap is 7/10 batters and 5/10 bowlers.

Calculation details

For each player, cell, metric and context, the observed match contributes a numerator x and a ball denominator n. In draw b, let wᵦₘ be the number of times match m is selected. Each resampled rate is Σₘ wᵦₘxₘ / Σₘ wᵦₘnₘ. Bowling economy multiplies that ratio by six. Subtract ordinary from pressure in each cell. Average those differences across retained cells weighted by their resampled pressure-ball counts. Convert each difference to a signed z-score using the observed matched cohort's mean and sample standard deviation, average the three z-scores, then apply nᵦ/(nᵦ+60), where nᵦ is the draw's pressure-ball total. For M valid sorted scores, quantile q is linearly interpolated at the zero-indexed position (M − 1)q.

PAPRF v4 reanalysis protocol

Four research passes

  1. Audit scoring attribution. Count all observed dismissal flags and separate striker events on a counted ball from non-striker events, events without a counted ball and events lacking an ID.
  2. Fit a regularised fine-cell model to the same pre-delivery pressure indicators. Compare outcomes within innings, phase and competition family. Use the historical 60-ball and six-match thresholds in both contexts, summed across overlapping cells.
  3. Recalculate under different regularisation and outcome assumptions and repeat the chronological test.
  4. Rebuild attribution from the organiser CSVs in a clean temporary directory, then recompute v4 and compare the resulting rankings and calculation ledgers byte for byte.

Prespecified model

Batting dismissal risk is the number of recorded striker dismissals on counted balls faced divided by those balls. This aligns the at-risk unit with the numerator. A non-striker dismissal still affects subsequent match state. Bowling retains credited wickets per legal ball and conceded runs per six legal balls, including attributed illegal-delivery runs. No outcome enters the pressure label for its own delivery.

Let a and b be ordinary and pressure opportunities in a player's cell. Require a > 0 and b > 0. With raw cell rate difference d, estimate the cell lift by minimising

(a b / (a + b)) × (theta − d)^2 + lambda × theta^2.

Differentiation gives theta = d × a b / [a b + lambda × (a + b)]. Thus small cells contribute an attenuated change. This is a zero-lift regulariser. It does not assume that an unobserved ordinary rate equals the player's observed pressure rate. Aggregate theta across supported cells with pressure-ball weights b. Use lambda = 12 for the primary model and test 6 and 24. Use observed role-peer mean and sample standard deviation for each aggregate component. Cap each signed z-score at ±3, give the three metrics equal weights and multiply the average by n/(n+60). This caps influence without deleting outlying observations. Test uncapped scores, historical dismissal attribution for batters and a two-component model that drops dismissal or wicket rate.

For the primary score, draw 500 whole-match samples within IPL and T20I with seed 20261118. Keep the observed qualifying cell set and peer scaling. A cell contributes in a resample only when both denominators are positive, and the pressure-ball denominator and shrinkage reflect the contributing cells in that draw. A draw with no overlapping opportunities is invalid. Require at least 475 valid draws. Rank by the 10th percentile using linear interpolation, six-decimal rounding and player ID as tie-break. Report the median, 90th percentile and conditional range. This conditional bootstrap does not reselect the model or estimate future performance.

PAPRF matched split-half reliability audit

Design recorded before evaluation

The forward test combines calendar drift with measurement variation. Here, 32 random splits interleave the calendar. For each split, shuffle whole matches within competition family and calendar year, then assign alternate matches to two disjoint halves. Randomise which half gets the first match in each stratum. This keeps adjacent deliveries and shared player appearances within a match together. Use seed 20261119. Evaluate the v4 point composite and individual components using the published full-sample peer scaling in both halves. This is a retrospective measurement diagnostic.

Rebuild both halves from opportunity counts, with a primary 30-ball and three-match gate in each context and a stricter 60-ball and six-match gate as sensitivity. The stricter gate filters the same scored halves so comparisons reflect coverage and composition. Use only player IDs with evidence on both sides. Compute Spearman correlation using midranks for both the composite and the signed component deltas. Top-ten membership uses the common pool and the same ID tie-break as v4.

For a descriptive null, sort common players by their full-sample pressure opportunity count and split them into three nearly equal exposure groups. Within each group, shuffle the second-half player assignments independently 200 times while preserving both halves' measured scores and their rank distributions. The first and second half samples themselves retain whole-match structure. This null breaks persistent player pairing conditional on these exposure groups. It does not create a new outcome data set or give a design-based significance test. Exposure strata do not control all differences in competition mix, roles, or shared match dependencies.

Report medians and 10th–90th percentile ranges across splits. Splits reuse matches, so these quantiles measure sensitivity to partition assignment. Do not treat 32 splits or 6,400 shuffles as independent realised seasons. Under the parallel-measurement assumption, the Spearman–Brown expression 2r/(1+r) translates a half-sample reliability r to a same-population full-sample heuristic. It is descriptive only because these halves differ in eligibility and support and Spearman correlation is rank based.
